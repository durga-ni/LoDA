# Day 2: Practice & Coaching on Real Use Cases

**Track:** From Prompting to Workflows

---

## Core idea

**Purpose:** Day 1 taught prompting patterns in the abstract, this day exists to prove they work on the participants' actual, messy, real work, not a clean invented example. Real problems surface real gaps in Day 1's patterns, which is exactly the point of this day.

The day is built around the participants' own repetitive or time-consuming work, not invented examples. They surface their own problems, then solve one for real, in the room, with coaching.

## Run of show



### 1. Use-case gathering (60 min total)

- 30 min: participants individually write down tasks they do repetitively, anything abundant or time-consuming, and one problem they'd genuinely want AI to help with. (with a well structured reflection activity/questions)
- 30 min: coach collects and scans all the inputs. Where two or more people surface a similar or strong enough problem, pair them up. Coaches pair directly with any problem that looks more critical or meaningfully different from the rest.



### 2. Coach demo (drawing on Day 1)

Pick one gathered problem and demo, live, how to work it through with AI: prompting it using the patterns from Day 1, holding a continuous back-and-forth rather than a single prompt-and-done.

### 3. Practice, in pairs, with coaches circulating for gotchas

**Purpose:** the describe-don't-prescribe constraint is the point of this step, not a side rule. Left to their own habits, people default to telling the AI how to solve the problem, which just re-encodes their own blind spots into the solution. Forcing them to describe the problem instead and let the AI ask questions is what surfaces the gaps they didn't know they had.

Each pair writes a long, complete prompt for their own use case: everything about the problem, how they currently do it manually, and every resource involved (spreadsheets, drive docs, meeting notes, codebase, whatever is relevant).

The deliberate constraint: describe the problem, do not prescribe the solution. Do not tell the AI how to solve it, only what the problem is and what currently happens manually. Let the AI ask its own clarifying questions and propose its own ideas first, then respond to those.

This is the core teaching moment of the day, brought back to three things anyone needs before they can start resolving a use case with AI:

1. **Workspace setup**, what would a new person on the team need in their environment to even attempt this problem.
2. **AI access setup**, giving the AI actual access to what it needs (e.g. through tool, an MCP connection, or equivalent), not just a text description.
3. **Describe, don't prescribe**, state the problem and the manual process, hand over every resource, and let the AI surface the missing pieces and options before you converge on a solution together.

From there: brainstorm with the AI on what's missing, what the real constraints and resources are, and converge on an approach.

**Mid-session showcase:** all pairs work on their own problems in parallel while coaches roam the room helping wherever needed. The pair a coach paired directly with in step 1, the one with the more critical or meaningfully different problem, gets end-to-end support: prompting, iterating, deciding an approach. That pair showcases mid-session what they decided and why, giving the room a second live worked example beyond the opening coach demo, this time from a peer rather than a coach.

### 4. Outcome of the exercise

The solved use case can land as a reusable skill, an agent, or simply a script that automates the manual work. Any of the three counts as a win.

### 5. Close of day

Two to three pairs demo the problem they worked and what they landed on, to the whole room, not just one. The point is cross-pollination: the room sees multiple different approaches to different problems, and coaches can draw out what worked, what didn't, and what advice applies across more than one pair's experience.
